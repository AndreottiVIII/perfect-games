Twenty official perfect games from 1908 to 2025, plus Galarraga’s.

Every regular-season game in which a team retired all 27 opposing batters without letting anyone reach base (a perfect game), as found in the Retrosheet data. The table is generated by our code straight from the data.

  • In 13 of the 20 official ones the last out is a pinch hitter: a substitute sent up to bat for a teammate, usually for the No. 9 hitter.
  • Strikeouts: from 3 for Addie Joss in 1908 to 14 for Sandy Koufax in 1965 and Matt Cain in 2012. The average is 9.3, against 6.5 expected in those seasons.
  • Pitch counts are reliable only from 1988: 107 a game on average, from 88 for David Cone to 125 for Matt Cain.
  • Six in three seasons, from 2010 to 2012; since 2013 just one, Domingo Germán in 2023. Is that cluster just luck? See the Torture test.

The two that are missing

Cy Young’s (Boston, May 5, 1904) came before our data, which start in 1908. Don Larsen (New York Yankees, October 8, 1956) threw his in the World Series, that is, in the postseason: here we count only the regular season.

Almost perfect

Harvey Haddix (1959) retired 36 batters in a row and Pedro Martínez (1995) 27, but their games went to extra innings and the perfect game broke up after that. Another 12 times it ended at exactly the 27th batter, one out away. The last column says whether the game still ended as a no-hitter, with no hits allowed.

How we worked it out

In the data a game is perfect when a pitching side retires 27 or more batters without letting anyone reach base and the game goes the distance. The program src/validate.py checks that the 20 we find are exactly the ones on MLB’s official list since 1908.

Pinch hitter: position 11 in Retrosheet’s play-by-play. Pitches: total pitches to the 27 batters, shown only from 1988, when pitch counts become complete. How: strikeout, caught in the air, or on the ground.

Player names come from Retrosheet’s rosters (with accents added by hand), team names as they were that season, ballparks from a hand-made table of Retrosheet codes (src/stadi_nomi.csv). Tables generated by src/build_site.py.

Data: Retrosheet, regular season 1908-2025. Code and results: github.com/AndreottiVIII/perfect-games.
