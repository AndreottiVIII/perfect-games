At Coors Field and Fenway Park long perfect starts are much rarer than normal; among today’s parks, Dodger Stadium sees more of them.

For each ballpark we count the games in which the pitching side retired at least 9 batters in a row from the start, once through the lineup with nobody on, and compare them with what we would expect. The expected figure is how many we would have seen in that park if it played like the average of the same season, with or without a DH (designated hitter).

  • Coors Field (Denver): 105 against 169 expected (−38%), and in 4,900 pitching sides not one run of 18 straight outs, where 6.1 were expected. There a batter reaches base 37.2% of the time, against 33.0% in the other parks since 1993.
  • Fenway Park (Boston): 411 against 521 expected (−21%), over 17,398 pitching sides, the biggest sample of all. Coors and Fenway are the two clearest cases, far beyond what chance can do even when you look at 72 parks.
  • The Ballpark in Arlington (Texas, from 1994) is tough on pitchers too: 91 against 130.
  • At the other end, Dodger Stadium (Los Angeles): 443 against 382 (+16%), the clearest among parks still in use. Among the old ones, the Polo Grounds in New York (1912-1963) stands out: 294 against 232 (+27%).
  • Exhibition Stadium (Toronto, 1977-1989) has the biggest gap (+49%), but on few games: take it with a grain of salt.
  • Coors Field, Fenway Park, Arlington and the Polo Grounds also passed the even/odd test on the Torture test page: the same direction, clearly, in even and in odd seasons.

How we worked it out

For each pitching side we check whether it retired at least 9 batters in a row from the start of the game. Each game’s expected value is the share of sides that managed it in the same season and with the same kind of lineup (DH or not); adding up the expected values of the games played in a park gives that park’s expected figure.

With 72 parks some big gaps show up by chance: that is why Coors Field and Fenway Park matter most, since their gaps are huge compared with what chance can produce.

Later check: we estimated each park’s effect separately on even and odd seasons. Coors Field, Fenway Park, the Polo Grounds, Arlington and Dodger Stadium go the same way, beyond chance, in both halves; across all parks, the even-season and odd-season estimates agree far more than luck would allow. What the data cannot say is whether the cause is the park itself or the home team. Full numbers in output/verifiche.md.
